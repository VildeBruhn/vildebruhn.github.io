## Data

In this exercise, we will use a classic dataset adapted from [Theriot _et al._ (2006)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/paleobiology/article/abs/late-quaternary-rapid-morphological-evolution-of-an-endemic-diatom-in-yellowstone-lake-wyoming/B28737E2C2B8FBEC5D0577B5757401A5) and [Hunt _et al._ (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/paleobiology/article/using-state-space-models-to-understand-trait-evolution-in-fossil-lineages/34A24238A3ADB9738AB287ED3C051C80). The dataset consist of trait measurmeants from the diatom _Stephanodiscus yellowstonensis_, which is endemic to Yellowstone Lake, Wyoming, USA. The phenotypic time series consists of 63 samples and covers approximately 14,000 years. For each sample, 50 individuals are measured (occasionally fewer). The measured traits on each individual are valve diameter ($\mu m$), the number of costae per valve, and the number of spines per valve. The ecological importance of valve spines is believed to be improvment of nutrient uptake and photosynthetic rate by affecting how diatoms sink through the water column. The size of diatoms (valve diameter) affects growth rate and harvesting of light and nutrients. The costae (ribs) absorb external pressures and help avoid deformation when the diatomes are predated [(Voje 2020)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/evo.13838).
